For a YouTube summary app on iPhone, Damas can turn a supported public video link into a transcript and built-in summary that you can edit and copy. For a lecture, use that overview as the starting point for checked study notes; revision questions require your own work or an optional external LLM step.

## Start with a supported source

Damas supports public, on-demand YouTube, Instagram and X video links up to 90 minutes. It does not support private, restricted or live content. You need iOS 18.6 or later, an internet connection, Premium and consent for AI processing. Submit only content you are authorized to process.

If the transcript contains unclear passages, check them against the recording first. Summarizing an inaccurate transcript can carry the same mistakes into a shorter text.

## Make a useful overview

1. Copy the eligible video link and provide it in Damas on your iPhone.
2. Use the available transcription and summary tasks, following the current app instructions and reviewing the AI consent.
3. Wait for processing. The source, audio and connection affect how long this takes; background processing and notifications help you return later.
4. Read the summary with the transcript or recording nearby.
5. Edit and copy your notes once you have checked the important points.

## Check what the shorter version leaves out

Look for the central claim, supporting reasons and any conditions or disagreements that affect the conclusion. Check names, dates and numbers in the source. If a detail matters to your work, retain it in a separate note rather than assuming a short summary includes everything.

Keep the original link with your notes. Do not quote the summary as though it were the speaker’s exact wording: verify direct quotations in the video. To work in another language, use the translation workflow and check the meaning before relying on a condensed account.

## Turn a lecture overview into study notes

Choose one lecture of up to 90 minutes and write down what you need to understand before processing it. After reading the built-in summary, return to the transcript for definitions, worked examples and exceptions. A summary can help you navigate the argument, but it cannot recover a diagram or formula that the speaker never explains aloud. Watch those sections in the source.

Use this example note structure, filling it only with information you have checked:

- Source: original link, lecture title and speaker as shown in the source.
- Main question: what problem does the lecture address?
- Core idea: explain it in your own words, keeping its conditions.
- Evidence or example: record the example actually used, including units.
- Uncertainty: mark unclear words or missing visual information for review.

## Optional: draft revision questions in another LLM

Copy the checked transcript into another LLM only if you are allowed to share it with that service. This is a separate external workflow, not a Damas integration or automatic flashcard feature. Example copy/paste prompt:

> Using only the transcript below, draft five revision questions with short answers. Include one question about an exception or limitation. Do not add outside facts. Mark missing answers as “not stated.” Separate paraphrases from exact quotations. Transcript: [paste checked text].

Check each answer against the video, then try answering without the notes. If you cannot explain an answer, revisit that section rather than memorizing the draft. Verify every quotation word for word and keep the source link alongside the finished notes.
